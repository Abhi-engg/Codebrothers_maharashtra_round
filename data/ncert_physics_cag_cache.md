## Chapter: Light - Reflection and Refraction

### 1. Reflection of Light
*   **Laws of Reflection:**
    1.  The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection ($i = r$).
    2.  The incident ray, the normal to the mirror at the point of incidence, and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.
*   **Spherical Mirrors:**
    *   **Concave Mirror:** Reflecting surface is curved inwards (towards the center of the sphere). Used in torches, searchlights, shaving mirrors, and dentist mirrors.
    *   **Convex Mirror:** Reflecting surface is curved outwards. Used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles because they give an erect, diminished image and a wider field of view.

### 2. Image Formation by Concave Mirror
*   Object at Infinity $\rightarrow$ Image at Focus $F$ (Highly diminished, Real and Inverted).
*   Object beyond $C$ $\rightarrow$ Image between $F$ and $C$ (Diminished, Real and Inverted).
*   Object at $C$ $\rightarrow$ Image at $C$ (Same size, Real and Inverted).
*   Object between $C$ and $F$ $\rightarrow$ Image beyond $C$ (Enlarged, Real and Inverted).
*   Object at $F$ $\rightarrow$ Image at Infinity (Highly enlarged, Real and Inverted).
*   Object between $P$ and $F$ $\rightarrow$ Image behind the mirror (Enlarged, **Virtual and Erect**).

### 3. Cartesian Sign Convention for Spherical Mirrors & Lenses
*   The Pole ($P$) / Optical Centre ($O$) is taken as the origin $(0,0)$.
*   The principal axis is the x-axis.
*   **Object Distance ($u$):** Always negative ($u < 0$) because the object is always placed to the left of the mirror/lens.
*   **Focal Length ($f$):**
    *   Concave Mirror / Concave Lens: **Negative** ($f < 0$).
    *   Convex Mirror / Convex Lens: **Positive** ($f > 0$).
*   **Image Distance ($v$):**
    *   Real image (formed in front of mirror / behind lens): $v$ is negative for mirrors, positive for lenses.
    *   Virtual image (formed behind mirror / in front of lens): $v$ is positive for mirrors, negative for lenses.
*   **Height:** Upward (perpendicular to principal axis) is positive ($h > 0$). Downward is negative ($h' < 0$).

### 4. Mirror Formula and Magnification
*   **Mirror Formula:** $\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$
*   **Magnification ($m$):** $m = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{v}{u}$
    *   If $m$ is negative $\rightarrow$ Image is real and inverted.
    *   If $m$ is positive $\rightarrow$ Image is virtual and erect.

### 5. Refraction of Light & Lenses
*   **Laws of Refraction:**
    1.  Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
    2.  **Snell's Law:** $\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \text{constant}$ (Refractive Index, $n$).
*   **Refractive Index ($n_m$):** $n_m = \frac{\text{Speed of light in vacuum (c)}}{\text{Speed of light in medium (v)}}$.
*   When light travels from rarer to denser medium, it bends **towards** the normal.
*   When light travels from denser to rarer medium, it bends **away from** the normal.

### 6. Lens Formula and Power
*   **Lens Formula:** $\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$
*   **Magnification ($m$):** $m = \frac{v}{u}$
*   **Power of a Lens ($P$):** $P = \frac{1}{f(\text{in meters})}$. Unit is Dioptre (D).
    *   Convex Lens Power = Positive.
    *   Concave Lens Power = Negative.

## Chapter: Electricity

### 1. Electric Current and Circuit
*   **Electric Current ($I$):** Rate of flow of electric charge. $I = \frac{Q}{t}$. Unit: Ampere (A).
*   Current flows from the positive terminal to the negative terminal (opposite to the flow of electrons).
*   Measured by an **Ammeter**, which is always connected in **series**.

### 2. Electric Potential and Potential Difference
*   **Potential Difference ($V$):** Work done to move a unit charge from one point to another. $V = \frac{W}{Q}$. Unit: Volt (V).
*   Measured by a **Voltmeter**, which is always connected in **parallel** across the points where PD is to be measured.

### 3. Ohm's Law and Resistance
*   **Ohm's Law:** At constant temperature, the current flowing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends. $V \propto I \implies V = IR$.
*   **Resistance ($R$):** Property of a conductor to resist the flow of charges. Unit: Ohm ($\Omega$).
*   **Factors affecting Resistance:**
    1.  Length ($l$): $R \propto l$.
    2.  Area of Cross-section ($A$): $R \propto \frac{1}{A}$.
    3.  Material (Resistivity $\rho$): $R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$.

### 4. Resistors in Series and Parallel
*   **Series Combination:**
    *   Current ($I$) is constant across all resistors.
    *   Total Voltage $V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$.
    *   Equivalent Resistance: $R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$.
    *   *Misconception Alert:* Current does NOT get "used up" across series resistors. It remains identical throughout the loop.
*   **Parallel Combination:**
    *   Potential Difference ($V$) is constant across all branches.
    *   Total Current $I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3$.
    *   Equivalent Resistance: $\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$.

### 5. Heating Effect of Electric Current (Joule's Law)
*   **Work Done / Heat Produced ($H$):** $H = VIt = I^2Rt = \frac{V^2}{R}t$. Unit: Joules (J).
*   **Electric Power ($P$):** Rate at which electrical energy is consumed. $P = VI = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}$. Unit: Watt (W).
*   **Commercial Unit of Energy:** Kilowatt-hour (kWh). $1 \text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6 \text{ Joules}$.
